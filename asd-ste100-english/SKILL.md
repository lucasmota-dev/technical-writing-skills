# ASD-STE100 Simplified Technical English

## Purpose

Write English technical content that readers can understand on the first read. Prefer clear, direct, specific language. Preserve the technical meaning, limits, warnings, and exceptions in the source.

Use this skill for manuals, procedures, help articles, troubleshooting steps, product documentation, interface text, technical notices, and similar content.

## Status and authority

ASD-STE100 is a controlled-language standard for technical English. The current reference identified by the official ASD website is Issue 9, dated 15 January 2025:

- Official website: https://www.asd-ste100.org/
- Official downloads and request page: https://www.asd-ste100.org/STE_downloads.html

This skill summarizes practical writing behavior. It does not reproduce the complete standard or its controlled dictionary. **Do not claim that a text fully complies with ASD-STE100 unless the applicable Issue 9 rules and dictionary have been checked.** If those materials are not available, describe the result as “STE-inspired” or “written in a simplified technical-English style.”

## Core rules

1. **Write for the reader and task.** Identify who will read the text, what they must understand or do, and what result they need.
2. **Use direct language.** Prefer common, concrete words over abstract, decorative, or bureaucratic language.
3. **Control vocabulary.** For strict STE work, check words against the official dictionary. Use each approved word only with its approved meaning and part of speech. Do not change words only to avoid repetition.
4. **Use exact technical terms.** Keep product names, interface labels, part names, commands, variables, identifiers, and domain terms consistent. Do not replace a precise term with a vague synonym.
5. **Keep one main idea in each sentence.** Split sentences when they contain several actions, conditions, or results.
6. **Prefer active voice.** Name the person, system, or component that performs the action. Use passive voice only when the actor is unknown or is not relevant.
7. **Make actions explicit.** Use a direct verb. Avoid phrases such as “it is necessary to,” “in order to,” and “the user should proceed to” when a simpler instruction works.
8. **Use simple sentence structures.** Avoid nested clauses, long noun groups, idioms, wordplay, and unnecessary parenthetical text.
9. **Use verb forms carefully.** Follow the official STE rules for verb forms and tenses. Do not assume that every form allowed in general English is allowed in STE.
10. **Make sequence and conditions clear.** State what to do first, when to do it, and what result to expect. Put conditions next to the actions they control.
11. **Make references specific.** Replace unclear words such as “it,” “this,” “that,” or “the above” when the reader could identify the wrong item.
12. **Keep numbers and units unambiguous.** Preserve approved product conventions, units, limits, formats, and precision. Do not calculate or change values unless requested and supported.
13. **Handle safety information carefully.** Keep the specified warning level and approved wording. State the hazard, consequence, and required action when the source provides them. Never invent or weaken a warning.
14. **Simplify without removing meaning.** Keep prerequisites, permissions, restrictions, failure cases, and exceptions. Technical accuracy has priority over shortness.

## Procedure-writing rules

For a procedure:

- Put the steps in the order the reader must perform them.
- Start each step with a clear action.
- Keep one main action in each step. Split a step when it requires unrelated actions.
- Name the exact screen, field, button, command, file, or component.
- State prerequisites before the steps that depend on them.
- State the expected result when it helps the reader confirm success.
- Use numbered steps for a required sequence and bullets for items without a required order.
- Keep warnings close to the step where the hazard can occur.
- Do not claim that an action succeeded unless the source confirms that result.

## Vocabulary and terminology

When an official STE dictionary is available, use it as the authority for general vocabulary. If a word is not approved, find a permitted alternative without changing the technical meaning. Technical nouns and technical verbs may be necessary; use recognized domain terms and define unfamiliar terms when needed.

Maintain a terminology list when the document set is large. Record the preferred term, meaning, allowed forms, and any terms that must not be used as synonyms. Reuse the list across related documents.

Do not invent an STE word list, rule, or approval. When a vocabulary decision cannot be verified, flag it for review rather than presenting it as compliant.

## Workflow

1. **Understand:** Identify the audience, purpose, document type, required actions, and technical constraints.
2. **Extract:** Preserve facts, values, names, UI labels, prerequisites, warnings, and exceptions.
3. **Draft:** Write direct sentences and clear steps. Use consistent terminology.
4. **Check language:** Remove ambiguity, unnecessary words, long noun groups, idioms, and avoidable passive voice.
5. **Check STE:** If the official materials are available, verify the applicable writing rules and dictionary entries. Otherwise, do not claim full compliance.
6. **Check meaning:** Compare the draft with the source. Confirm that no requirement, limitation, or technical fact changed.
7. **Deliver:** Return the finished text in the requested format. Add notes only when an unresolved ambiguity or a compliance limitation matters.

## Final review checklist

- Can the intended reader identify the action or main point on the first read?
- Does each sentence express one main idea?
- Are the actor, action, object, condition, and expected result clear where needed?
- Are terms and interface labels consistent?
- Are the words verified against the official dictionary when strict STE compliance is requested?
- Are sequence, values, units, warnings, limits, and exceptions preserved?
- Did the rewrite add a cause, behavior, or promise that the source does not support?
- Is any compliance claim accurate and verifiable?

## Output behavior

- When asked to **write**, produce the requested content directly.
- When asked to **rewrite**, retain the original intent and all material facts while improving clarity.
- When asked to **review**, report only meaningful issues and show specific corrections.
- When the source is ambiguous, do not guess about technical behavior. Ask one focused question if needed, or mark the unresolved assumption clearly.
- Do not add introductory commentary, lengthy explanations, or alternative versions unless requested.
